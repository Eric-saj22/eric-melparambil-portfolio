01 · First version · QQQ, 1-hour bars

QQQ High-Win-Rate Micro-Scalp

Buys extreme short-term dips while price is above its 50-period average, then takes a small, quick profit so that most trades win. The stop is twice the size of the target, so the win rate has to stay high.

Why I moved onI wanted to run the idea under FTMO’s rules, and FTMO doesn’t offer QQQ or options. So I rebuilt it on SPY as an S&P 500 proxy.

  • Entry RSI(5) below 15, price above SMA(50)
  • Exit RSI(5) above 50
  • Risk 1 ATR stop, 0.5 ATR target

02 · FTMO rebuild · SPY, 5-minute bars

SPY RSI-2 Mean Reversion Micro-Scalp

The same idea moved to SPY and faster bars: buy short-term oversold dips only while the 50-period average is above the 200-period average, and exit once momentum reverts. A hard stop caps the trades that don’t revert.

03 · Refinement · SPY, 5-minute bars

SPY RSI-2 Micro-Scalp, Version 3

What I changedI loosened the entry from RSI(2) below 10 to below 15 and took profit sooner, at RSI(2) above 60 instead of 65. Trades rose from 92 to 138 and Sharpe from 1.35 to 1.80, with a slightly deeper drawdown.

Why I used itFrequent small wins with a hard stop keep drawdowns small, which matters under a prop firm’s loss limits.

Why I moved on138 trades in 12 weeks still wasn’t enough, so I moved to a 1-minute scalper to trade far more often.

04 · Trading strategy · AAPL, 1-minute bars

1-Min Trend Pullback Scalper

Inspired by a day-trading momentum strategy and built as an addition to the micro-scalp approach, with more focus on market direction: reading whether the market was bullish or bearish from the previous week and the 4-hour chart. This Astral version is a simplified, long-only copy of my Python scalping bot that leaves out the trend filter. Neither version is validated as profitable yet.

Why I chose itI needed much higher trade frequency. Moving to 1-minute bars delivered 587 round trips in under six weeks of paper trading.

What it showedThe backtest lost money even before costs. The later paper run was profitable, returning +0.79% on a $10,000 account, but the paper account charges no trading costs. Hypothetically, with no trip-ups, meaning nothing held overnight and every stop and target filled at its set level, it would have returned +1.59%. In a real funded account, trading costs and slippage would have wiped that out: at 1 basis point per side, it becomes a loss of about 9.7%.

Why I moved onI moved to 5-minute bars: fewer trades and a bigger target on each, so costs take a smaller share of every trade.

  • Entry RSI(2) below 20
  • Exit RSI(2) above 65, or 15:55 flatten rule
  • Risk 0.25% stop, 0.5% target

Why they don’t match: trades left open overnight7 of the 74 paper trades stayed open overnight, even though the strategy is meant to close by 15:55. They account for $539 of the $954 loss, including a single −6.0% gap on 22 Oct.

Why they don’t match: latency on Astral11 of the 74 entries filled more than a minute after the bar close, while the backtest fills at the close. 23 same-day trades also closed past the 0.5% stop, the worst at −1.24%. I also noticed times when trades didn’t seem to be logged.

What I plan to fix in the next strategyForce every position flat before the close and block late entries, then build fill delays and stop slippage into the backtest and check the paper log against the expected signals. Same-day trades alone still lost about $415, so these two issues aren’t the whole gap.
